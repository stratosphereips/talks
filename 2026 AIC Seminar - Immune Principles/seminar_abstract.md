# Seminar Title

What AI and Cybersecurity Can Learn from the Immune System

## Short Description

Modern AI and cybersecurity systems must make decisions from noisy, incomplete, and adversarial signals without causing damage through overreaction. This seminar argues that the biological immune system is interesting not because it offers a few metaphors, but because it offers design principles for decision-making under uncertainty. Starting from a recent paper on immunity principles for cybersecurity, the talk introduces a small set of transferable ideas: distributed sensing, context propagation, multi-signal activation, adaptive aggressiveness, memory, and regulated shutdown.

The seminar then shows why much of earlier cyber-immune research copied isolated mechanisms and missed the larger architecture. It closes with an implementation-oriented view of how these principles are being transferred into Stratosphere Linux IPS (SLIPS), including evidence signal classification, shared LLM-backed detector generation, and safer validation of generated signatures.

## Audience Fit

- General AI and IT audience
- No background in immunology required
- No background in cybersecurity required

## Key Takeaway

Nature-inspired research is most useful when it transfers control principles and system architecture, not surface analogies.
